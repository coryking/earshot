# Problem statement

Radio is organized by frequency. I want to follow conversations, people, and events.

## Following what's happening

1. **Too much happens at once, and I can only listen to one thing.** The spectrum is wide and busy, and I can follow one conversation at a time. Something interesting is almost always going on somewhere I'm not listening.
2. **Scanners make me choose between missing things and whiplash.** Park on one channel and I miss everything else. Let it scan and I get scraps of a dozen conversations, none of them followable.
3. **I always arrive in the middle.** Live radio has no rewind. By the time a transmission sounds interesting, its start is gone.
4. **A conversation is a string of short transmissions with silence in between.** Every key-up is its own burst, and the reply may come seconds later, much later, or never. A scanner leaves during the gaps, other traffic fills them on a busy channel, and nothing ties the bursts back into one exchange.
5. **The story doesn't stay on one frequency.** Flights get handed from clearance to ground to tower to departure to center. Incidents move from dispatch to a working channel. Nets change frequency. Trunking systems say where the conversation went; almost nothing else does.
6. **Often I only hear one side.** I hear the tower but not the plane, or dispatch but not the unit out of range. Some pieces of the conversation never reach me at all.
7. **I can't tell what's worth hearing until I've heard it.** What makes something interesting is what's being said, not which channel it's on. Priority channels are a fixed guess made ahead of time.

## Knowing what's out there

8. **Setting up to listen is a chore.** Before hearing anything I have to work out what's used in my area, where it sits, and what it's called, then program it in. I redo it whenever things change.
9. **When something won't decode, I need to catch it and tinker with it.** Unknown and undocumented signals go by and they're gone. Working on one means recording the right slice of spectrum at the right moment and keeping it somewhere I can come back to. The rare ones make this worst, since there may not be a second chance.
